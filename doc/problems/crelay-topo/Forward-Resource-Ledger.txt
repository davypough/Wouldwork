CRELAY-TOPO FORWARD RESOURCE LEDGER
===================================

Purpose
-------

This is a bookkeeping aid for constructing and checking a forward solution.  It
does not assert an exact action order.  Its main job is to prevent a locally
useful placement from quietly consuming something that is needed later.

The problem contains two coupled executions once the recorder starts:

  live side       agent1, box1, connector1, tray1, ordinary gate states
  recording side  ghost-agent1, ghost-box1, ghost-connector1, ghost-tray1,
                  recording gate states

The shadows begin at the corresponding live objects' locations when
START-RECORDER is performed.  A forward candidate must therefore record both
where each resource is and which side of the playback it belongs to.


Notation
--------

  A    agent1                         A*   ghost-agent1
  B    box1                           B*   ghost-box1
  C    connector1                     C*   ghost-connector1
  T    tray1                          T*   ghost-tray1

  held     committed to a plate, beam station, support, or carried load
  free     recoverable without first undoing another required condition
  across   already beyond the named gate before that gate is allowed to close

For every proposed action, record its effect separately in the live and
recording views.  In particular, an ordinary OPEN fact is not automatically a
RECORDING-OPEN fact, and a beam arrangement must activate the receiver in the
view used by the agent that is to pass gate8.  The validator, rather than this
ledger, is the final authority on cross-side effects.

-----------------

Immediately after START-RECORDER:

  A* is at location1
  B* is at location6
  C* is at location9
  T* is at location7 and is forked onto plate2 in the recording view
  ordinary gate3 and recording gate3 are open in the checked opening state

The opening prefix checked against the staged problem is:

  START-RECORDER by A
  A* moves from location1 to location2
  A* steps onto plate1

This opens gate1 in both views in that tested state and permits A to leave the
starting enclosure.  The full first-cycle closure below now establishes how A*
is released and how C permanently replaces it at this checkpoint.


First closed-cycle subgoal -- validated
---------------------------------------

Target checkpoint:

  one recorder cycle has ended by a normal ghost STOP
  A is back at recorder1 at location1
  C is on plate1 at location2 and gate1 is open
  T is free on the ground at location5 for the box1 subgoal
  B remains at elevated location6 and gate2 remains open

Exact action sequence accepted by VALIDATE-SOLUTION:

   1. START-RECORDER A
   2. A* walks location1 -> location2
   3. A* steps from ground onto plate1
   4. A walks location1 -> location9 through gates1 and 3
   5. A picks up C at location9
   6. A walks location9 -> location2 through gates3 and 1
   7. A* steps from plate1 to ground
   8. A puts C on plate1
   9. A walks location2 -> location7 through gate1
  10. A picks up T from plate2 at location7
  11. A walks location7 -> location5
  12. A puts T on the ground at location5
  13. A walks location5 -> location1 through gate1
  14. A* walks location2 -> location1
  15. STOP-RECORDER A*

Validated final facts include DEPRESSED plate1, ON C plate1, OPEN gate1,
HAS-LOCATION T location5, HAS-LOCATION B location6, RECORDER-CYCLES-USED 1,
RECORDER-CYCLE-CLOSED, and RECORDER-CYCLE-STOPPED-BY-GHOST.  Gate3 is closed
because the cycle deliberately reclaims T from plate2 after C has crossed it.
The recorder-specific whole-cycle validator also returned VALID T with no
diagnostic.

The explicit pickup and placement of T are important.  A shorter 11-action
candidate that left T untouched also executed, but recorder forking had already
displaced the live ON T plate2 fact when T* was forked onto that same fixed
support.  The 15-action sequence gives T's final ground state a real live-side
cause instead of relying on that representation defect.

A 17-action continuation probe (the 15 actions above, then a second
START-RECORDER and A* walking location1 -> location5 through gate1) was fully
executable.  It confirms that the next ghost can reach the tray/box area.
Nevertheless, the second start similarly replaces live ON C plate1 with ON C*
plate1.  This is an unresolved interaction between recorder forking and ON's
bijective fixed-support index.  Do not yet assume that C remains physically on
plate1 after the second cycle closes.


Gate and station ledger
-----------------------

  gate/site  requirement                    persistence needed
  ---------  -----------------------------  ---------------------------------
  gate1      plate1 occupied                until start-side resources are out
  gate2      switch1 off                    while recovering elevated boxes
  blower1    switch1 on                     only if a deliberate blow is needed
  gate3      plate2 occupied                until all mid-puzzle resources pass
  gate4      plate3 occupied                while transmitter sightline is used
  gate5      switch2 off                    until post-switch staging is done
  gate6      plates4 and 5 both occupied    for the empty-handed switch2 trip
  screen1    crossed empty-handed           same trip as gate6
  gate7      switch2 on                     after the one-way phase change
  gate8      receiver1 active               until every required resource passes
  gate9      plates6, 7, and 8 occupied     while A walks to location19

Switch2 is the principal phase boundary:

  switch2 off  -> gate5 open, gate7 closed
  switch2 on   -> gate5 closed, gate7 open

Before turning switch2 on, everything that will be needed on its far side must
already be staged beyond gate5.  Treat the switch as irreversible for planning
purposes unless a complete and validated return route proves otherwise.


Commitment register
-------------------

  id  commitment                         release condition
  --  ---------------------------------  -------------------------------------
  K1  one occupant on plate1             A and needed cargo are beyond gate1
  K2  one occupant on plate2             all beam/post-switch cargo is past gate3
  K3  one occupant on plate3             all intended gate8 crossings are done
  K4  box plus connector at location9,   all intended gate8 crossings are done
      with connector paired to both
      transmitter1 and receiver1
  K5  two occupants on plates4 and 5     switch2 operator has returned through
                                         screen1 and gate6
  K6  three non-A occupants on plates6,  A has crossed gate9 to location19
      7, and 8

K3 and K4 together are the known candidate beam installation.  In a constructed
staged state, tray1 on plate3 at location10 opened gate4, while connector1 on
box1 at location9 and paired to transmitter1 and receiver1 activated receiver1
and opened gate8.  A ground-level connector at location9 was too low.

The item occupying plate3 need not literally be T; the exact live/ghost
allocation remains to be discovered.  Likewise, B and C may have to be replaced
by their shadow counterparts for a recording-side crossing.  Do not transfer
the checked geometry from one view to the other without replay validation.


Resource arithmetic
-------------------

After the recorder starts there are eight mobile resources:

  A, A*, B, B*, C, C*, T, T*

At the late peak, a conservative reservation is:

  1  A, free to make the goal move
  2  a box and connector committed to the beam station
  1  an occupant committed to plate3
  3  occupants committed to plates6, 7, and 8
  1  remaining mobile resource
  -  --------------------------------------------
  8  total

So the raw count is sufficient, but only by respecting location, side, carrying,
height, and reachability constraints.  This count is a necessary check, not a
proof of feasibility.

If the final goal is reached during the last recording cycle, that cycle may
remain open: the present problem goal does not require GHOST-STOPS-RECORDER.
This makes A* available as a possible final plate occupant.  If an intermediate
cycle is ended, however, its ghost must return to a valid recorder boundary or
the cycle must otherwise close cleanly and validate.


Provisional forward phases
--------------------------

PHASE 0 -- Fork the execution and open gate1

  Validated full cycle:
    A* opens gate1 from plate1.
    A retrieves C through initially open gate3 and puts C on plate1.
    A frees T from plate2 and stages it at location5.
    A and A* return to recorder1 and A* stops the recorder.

  Exit condition:
    C holds plate1, gate1 is open, T is free at location5, A is at recorder1,
    and the first recorder cycle is normally closed.


PHASE 1 -- Recover the elevated box resources

  Keep switch1 off and gate2 open.  Use the now-free T at location5 as the
  candidate standing support for reaching location6 and recovering B.  Determine
  separately whether B* must also be recovered.  Do not toggle switch1 merely
  because it is accessible.  Blower1 is useful only if the forward plan identifies
  an exact resource, source, and destination for the blow.

  Exit condition:
    The required box or boxes are recoverable on the intended side of gate2.


PHASE 2 -- Open gate3 and stage the middle resources

  T no longer occupies plate2 after Phase 0, so recommit one resource to plate2.
  Move every resource needed for the beam,
  plates3-5, the switch2 trip, and the final plate allocation beyond gate3 before
  releasing plate2.

  Exit condition:
    The complete post-gate3 inventory has crossed in the necessary view or views.


PHASE 3 -- Establish the beam station

  Candidate live-side installation:
    support box at location9
    connector on the box
    connector paired with transmitter1 and receiver1
    plate3 occupied so gate4 is open

  Record separately whether ordinary receiver1, recording receiver1, or both are
  active.  Hold K3 and K4 until every planned gate8 crossing in the corresponding
  view has occurred.

  Exit condition:
    The receiver state needed later has been demonstrated by replay, not inferred.


PHASE 4 -- Stage everything beyond gate5

  Gate5 begins open.  Move all agents and cargo required for plates4-8, gate8,
  and the goal into the post-gate5 region before operating switch2.

  Exit condition:
    The pre-switch inventory audit has no required item left behind gate5.


PHASE 5 -- Make the switch2 phase change

  Occupy plates4 and 5 to open gate6.  The switch operator crosses gate6 and
  screen1 empty-handed, reaches switch2 at location14, turns switch2 on, and
  returns while both plates remain held.  Reclaim the two plate occupants only
  after the operator is back on the useful side of gate6.

  Expected result:
    gate5 closed
    gate7 open
    both K5 occupants free again

  Exit condition:
    The operator and reusable resources are correctly placed for gate7.


PHASE 6 -- Transfer through gates7 and 8

  Pass gate7 under switch2.  Keep the appropriate receiver active while every
  agent and resource needed for the final plate arrangement passes gate8.

  Exit condition:
    A and three suitable non-A plate occupants are beyond gate8, and abandoning
    the beam cannot strand anything still required for the goal.


PHASE 7 -- Pay the final three-occupant cost

  Put three non-A resources on plates6, 7, and 8.  Confirm that gate9 is open in
  the view used by A.  A steps off any support or plate it occupies, walks through
  gate9, and reaches location19.

  Exit condition:
    The declared problem goal is true.


PHASE 8 -- Close an intermediate recording cycle, if one is used

  This phase is not required after a final open cycle that already reaches the
  goal.  It is required whenever the proposed solution ends an earlier cycle.

  With early gates closed and the ghost empty-handed, the checked directed ladder
  return is:

    location11 --ladder3--> location10
    location10 --walk-----> location8
    location8  --ladder2--> location5
    location5  --walk-----> location3
    location3  --ladder1--> location1

  The three ladders therefore remain reserved as an empty-handed recorder-return
  chain rather than being treated as decorative props.

Open allocation decisions
-------------------------

  1. How should recorder ON-forking be corrected so C remains on plate1 across
     later cycle starts and endings?
  2. Must both B and B* be recovered, or can one remain unused?
  3. Which side supplies the box, connector, and plate3 occupant for gate8?
  4. Which exact resources are staged beyond gate5 before switch2 is turned on?
  5. Which three non-A resources occupy plates6, 7, and 8?
  6. Is one final open recording cycle sufficient, or are intermediate closed
     cycles useful enough to justify the return cost?
  7. Does any proposed cross-side handoff actually validate under the recorder
     rules, or has it only been assumed in the paper plan?


Known non-resources and non-pruning conclusions
-----------------------------------------------

  repeater1
    Keep it because it is part of the given problem.  No useful route has been
    found.  Among the authored locations, only elevated location9 can see both
    repeater1 and receiver1, and it can also see transmitter1 directly.  A real
    repeater route would appear to require a second elevated connector and a new
    receiver-side location, making it strictly more expensive than the direct
    candidate.

  ladders1-3
    Keep all three.  They form the checked one-way return chain above.

  locations1-20
    Keep all for now.  Each currently has a distinct fixture, cargo, traversal,
    phase-boundary, or goal role.  Prune a location only after a complete forward
    allocation shows that its role is dominated by another reachable location.


Validation checkpoints
----------------------

For a candidate forward solution, validate in this order:

  [x] The exact 15-action first recorder cycle succeeds and reaches its checkpoint.
  [ ] Each release of plates1 and 2 leaves no required resource behind.
  [ ] Every box required by the allocation is actually recoverable before gate2
      can close.
  [ ] The beam station opens gate8 in the required live/recording view.
  [ ] Every post-switch resource is beyond gate5 before switch2 is turned on.
  [ ] The plate4/plate5 operator returns before either occupant is reclaimed.
  [ ] All final resources pass gate8 before the beam is abandoned.
  [ ] Three independent occupants remain on plates6-8 while A reaches location19.
  [ ] Every completed recorder cycle validates independently.
  [ ] The complete playback path reaches the problem goal.
